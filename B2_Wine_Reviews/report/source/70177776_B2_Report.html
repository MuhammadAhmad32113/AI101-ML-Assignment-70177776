1. Introduction

Sommelier ratings and wine critic scores heavily dictate pricing power, brand equity, and auction valuations within the global viticulture industry. In this study, we predict numerical wine critic scores (80–100 points) from natural language sensory descriptions, bottle pricing, and geographic origin metadata utilizing the WineEnthusiast benchmark corpus. Following mandatory de-duplication to eliminate test set contamination, we benchmark linear and non-linear regressors across structured, free-text, and combined modalities. Our central finding establishes that Ridge regression on combined modalities achieves the lowest error (MAE = 1.1875 points, $R^2 = 0.7580$), cutting metadata-only error by 37.5% and confirming that sommelier tasting notes provide substantial predictive variance beyond price and pedigree.

2. Problem Statement

Given an input vector $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_{ ext{meta}}, \mathbf{x}_{ ext{text}})$ consisting of numeric price attributes, categorical terroir indicators, and unstructured tasting notes, we formulate a supervised continuous regression task to estimate $y \in [80, 100]$, representing critic rating points. Performance is assessed via Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and the coefficient of determination ($R^2$). A naive mean-prediction baseline achieves MAE = 2.5290 with $R^2 = 0.0000$. The primary challenge lies in extracting fine-grained sensory descriptors from noisy narrative text while mitigating severe regression to the mean at distribution extremities.

3. Dataset Used & De-duplication

We utilize the Wine Reviews corpus (Kaggle: zynicide/wine-reviews; winemag-data-130k-v2.csv) comprising 129,971 raw reviews across 13 columns. De-duplication is critical: a pre-split audit identified 9,983 exact duplicate reviews (7.68% of the raw corpus). Without de-duplication, 1,573 test samples (12.1%) would leak directly from training partitions, yielding invalid overfitted scores. After removing all duplicates, 119,988 clean records remain. Features include numeric bottle price, missing price flags, one-hot geographical origin (top 15 countries, top 20 provinces), variety (top 25), and 10,000 sublinear TF-IDF unigram/bigram tokens from sommelier tasting descriptions.

5. Methodology

5.1 Preprocessing Pipeline & Feature Modalities

To enforce strict zero data leakage, transformations are assembled via ColumnTransformer. Missing bottle prices are imputed with training median $\log(1+ ext{price})$ while encoding a separate binary missingness indicator (price_missing). Categorical metadata are mapped to top frequency categories with rare labels coalesced into an "Other" bin. Textual descriptions are lowercased, stripped of English stop words, and transformed into 10,000 TF-IDF features with sublinear term-frequency weighting.

5.2 Learning Setting & Architectures

We benchmark four learning paradigms: (i) Baseline Mean Regressor, (ii) Ridge Regression ($lpha=1.0$), (iii) Linear Support Vector Regression (LinearSVR), and (iv) LightGBM Gradient Boosted Trees (250 estimators, learning rate 0.05, max depth 7). All models are evaluated across three distinct input configurations: Metadata Only, Text Only, and Combined.

6.1 Modality Contribution & Ablation Analysis

The controlled ablation across feature sets highlights the dominance of sensory language over metadata. Metadata alone yields an $R^2$ of only 0.4050 (MAE = 1.8987). Incorporating tasting descriptions drops MAE by 0.7112 points (−37.5%) and propels $R^2$ to 0.7580 (+87.2% relative variance explained). Text features capture nuanced sensory dimensions (tannin grip, acidity, length) that categorical origin and price cannot fully parameterize.

6.2 MAE Across Rating Bands & Regression to Mean

Table IV reveals error stratification across score intervals. The model attains lowest error in the high-density middle band (85–89 points: MAE = 1.1522). Conversely, error widens sharply at the tails: 80–84 points exhibits MAE = 2.2370 (mean actual 83.14 vs. predicted 85.30), while 95–100 points exhibits MAE = 2.8113 (mean actual 95.66 vs. predicted 92.89). This phenomenon is driven by regression to the mean: squared error loss penalizes rare outlier estimates, pulling extreme predictions inward toward the central mean ($\sim$88.45).

6.3 Interpretability: Ridge Lexical Coefficients

Ridge weights reveal words driving score predictions. Positively weighted tokens include: gorgeous (+7.32), superb (+7.11), delicious (+6.63), beautiful (+6.25), stunning (+5.98), complex (+5.79), and extraordinary (+5.75). Conversely, strongly negative weights penalize defects: lacks (−5.75), weird (−4.41), disappointing (−4.17), sour (−4.17), puckering (−4.10), watery (−3.92), simple (−3.23), and past prime (−3.13).

7. Conclusion

Multimodal regression fusing TF-IDF descriptions with log-price and origin metadata accurately estimates wine critic scores, attaining an MAE of 1.1875 points and explaining 75.8% of variance via Ridge regression. De-duplication is vital to prevent 12.1% test data leakage. Future work should investigate deep domain-specific fine-tuning (e.g. SommelierBERT) and ordinal ranking formulations.
