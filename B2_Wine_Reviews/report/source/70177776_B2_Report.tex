\begin{abstract}
Sommelier ratings and wine critic scores heavily dictate pricing and auction valuations. In this work, we predict wine scores (80--100 points) from natural language sensory descriptions and metadata. After rigorous de-duplication removing 9,983 duplicate reviews, Ridge regression on combined multimodal features achieves the lowest prediction error of MAE = 1.1875 ($R^2 = 0.7580$), reducing metadata-only error by 37.5\% and demonstrating that sommelier tasting notes contain significant predictive signal beyond price and origin.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Multimodal Regression, NLP, Wine Ratings, Ridge Regression, De-duplication.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Wine critic points dictate market demand. We utilize the 130k Wine Reviews dataset to model ratings via combined metadata and free-text representations.

\section{Problem Statement}
Given $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_{\text{meta}}, \mathbf{x}_{\text{text}})$, we learn $f(\mathbf{x}) \to y \in [80, 100]$. Evaluation metrics are MAE, RMSE, and $R^2$.

\section{Dataset and De-duplication}
The Wine Reviews corpus contains 129,971 raw reviews. Exact de-duplication removed 9,983 duplicates (7.7\%), preventing 12.1\% test-set leakage. The clean dataset contains 119,988 records partitioned 80/10/10.

\section{Methodology and Results}
TF-IDF (10,000 features) was combined with log-price and categorical origin features. Models evaluated: Dummy Baseline, Ridge ($\alpha=1.0$), LinearSVR, and LightGBM.

Ridge (Combined) achieved the best test MAE of 1.1875 and $R^2$ of 0.7580. An error band analysis revealed regression to the mean at extreme rating bands (80--84 MAE = 2.2370; 95--100 MAE = 2.8113).

\section{Conclusion}
Sensory descriptions provide crucial predictive information, significantly enhancing price and origin metadata alone.
