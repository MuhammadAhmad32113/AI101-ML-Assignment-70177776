1. Introduction

Online recruitment fraud presents grave socioeconomic threats, exposing vulnerable applicants to identity theft, financial extortion, and malicious phishing campaigns while eroding confidence in digital employment portals. Automated screening of job listings requires processing diverse information modalities simultaneously: free-form recruitment descriptions, structured compensation criteria, and company credentials. In this work, we analyze the EMSCAD benchmark comprising 17,880 employment postings. We construct a leakage-free multimodal classification framework integrating sublinear TF-IDF n-gram representations with engineered missingness flags and categorical metadata. Our primary finding demonstrates that fusing structured metadata with free-text yields a peak fraud F1-score of 0.8907 and PR-AUC of 0.9555 via Calibrated Linear SVM, exceeding single-modality structured predictors by +90.3% relative F1 gain.

2. Problem Statement

Formally, each observation is represented as a tuple $\mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{x}_{ ext{struct}}, \mathbf{x}_{ ext{text}})$, where $\mathbf{x}_{ ext{struct}} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ contains categorical, binary, and missingness attributes, and $\mathbf{x}_{ ext{text}}$ denotes concatenated textual narratives. We learn a parameterized discriminant $f: \mathbf{x} o y \in \{0, 1\}$, where $y=1$ signifies a fraudulent job advert and $y=0$ denotes a verified authentic advert. The task exhibits severe class imbalance, with positive fraud cases accounting for approximately 4.84% of observations. In this setting, the cost of false negatives (unflagged scams causing monetary loss or legal liability) greatly outweighs false positives. Standard classification accuracy is inherently misleading: a trivial baseline predicting "real" uniformly achieves 95.15% accuracy while completely failing to detect any fraudulent postings. Consequently, system efficacy is evaluated via minority-class Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Precision-Recall Area Under Curve (PR-AUC).

3. Dataset Used

We utilize the University of the Aegean Employment Scam Aegean Dataset (EMSCAD; Kaggle: shivamb/real-or-fake-fake-jobposting-prediction) published under CC BY-SA 4.0. The dataset contains 17,880 total job postings spanning 17 raw features: five natural language narratives (title, company profile, description, requirements, benefits), five categorical attributes (employment type, required experience, required education, industry, function), and three binary status flags (telecommuting, has company logo, has questions). Text preprocessing strips HTML entities, lowercases tokens, and extracts 20,000 unigram/bigram TF-IDF features with sublinear term-frequency scaling. Explicit binary missingness indicators are constructed across all attributes, recognizing that omission of organizational information serves as a powerful predictive marker.

5. Methodology

5.1 Learning Setting & Modality Fusion

We deploy sklearn.compose.ColumnTransformer inside integrated pipelines to prevent information leakage from validation and test sets. Numerical features (character lengths, word counts) are imputed with constant zeros and scaled via StandardScaler. Categorical variables are imputed with missingness categories and one-hot encoded (top 15 levels per column). Textual fields are concatenated and vectorized into 20,000 TF-IDF features. We compare four distinct classifiers: (i) Balanced Logistic Regression (L-BFGS), (ii) Calibrated Linear SVM via 3-fold Platt scaling, (iii) Balanced Random Forest (100 trees), and (iv) LightGBM with scale_pos_weight = 19.7.

5.2 Data Split Approach & Imbalance Mitigation

A stratified 70/15/15 train/validation/test split is applied using fixed seed 42. To handle severe 1:20 class imbalance without introducing synthetic artifacts, cost-sensitive inverse class weighting is strictly enforced on the training set. Furthermore, optimal decision thresholds $ au^*$ are swept exclusively across validation probability scores to maximize fraud F1-score, and then frozen for final test evaluation.

6.1 Mandatory Modality Ablation Study

Table IV presents the controlled ablation study across structured features, free-text features, and combined inputs using Logistic Regression. Structured metadata alone achieves an F1 of only 0.4378 (PR-AUC 0.4167) due to limited semantic granularity. Text features alone effectively capture vocabulary cues, attaining F1 = 0.8199 and PR-AUC = 0.9201. Merging both modalities elevates F1 to 0.8333 (a +90.3% relative improvement over structured data alone and +1.6% over text alone). This empirically confirms that missingness signals and lexical patterns provide complementary discriminative evidence.

6.2 Qualitative Error Analysis & Salient Signals

Model coefficient inspection reveals distinct fraud-associated lexical markers: link url (+3.08), earn (+2.73), money (+2.66), aptitude staffing (+2.63), data entry (+2.14), and cash (+1.79). Among structured indicators, omission of organizational profile (miss_company_profile) and lack of company logo (has_company_logo=0) constitute the most pronounced non-textual indicators. False-positive scrutiny indicates that authentic postings are misflagged primarily when legitimate employers fail to provide corporate profiles or when brief descriptions mimic remote freelance boilerplate.

7. Conclusion

Multimodal integration of free-text TF-IDF representations and structured metadata provides superior detection of fraudulent job postings, reaching an F1-score of 0.8907. Calibrated Linear SVM coupled with validation threshold tuning provides the strongest defense against severe class imbalance. Future explorations should evaluate contextual transformer architectures (e.g. RoBERTa) and applicant graph network linkages.
