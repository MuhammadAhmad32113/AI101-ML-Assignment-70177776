Abstract —
Abstractive text summarisation condenses multi-sentence journalistic documents into single-sentence semantic syntheses without verbatim sentence copying. Unlike extractive benchmarks, the BBC Extreme Summarisation (XSum) dataset features professionally authored, highly compressed single-sentence summaries exhibiting an unprecedented 56.4% novel bigram rate. We evaluate three distinct architectures: (i) an extractive Lead-1 baseline, (ii) a custom-trained Bidirectional GRU Encoder-Decoder with Bahdanau additive attention, and (iii) a fine-tuned sequence-to-sequence Transformer (T5-small). Evaluated on 1,000 held-out test articles, Lead-1 achieves poor overlap (ROUGE-1: 16.32%, ROUGE-2: 3.15%, ROUGE-L: 11.94%) due to XSum's non-extractive lead structure. The custom GRU achieves ROUGE-1 of 28.45% and ROUGE-L of 22.15%, yet suffers from exposure bias and phrase repetition (average repeated trigram ratio: 0.0825). Fine-tuned T5-small with beam search and trigram blocking (no_repeat_ngram_size=3) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics (ROUGE-1: 37.18%, ROUGE-2: 14.65%, ROUGE-L: 29.82%), eliminating repetition while exhibiting a 15% factual hallucination rate upon rigorous manual verification.
Keywords: Abstractive Summarisation, XSum, Bahdanau Attention, T5-small, ROUGE Metrics, Beam Search, Hallucination Analysis.

I. Introduction & Problem Statement

Text summarisation constitutes a core sequence-to-sequence problem within natural language processing, categorized broadly into extractive and abstractive paradigms. Extractive methods score and select salient sentences directly from the source text, excelling on corpora with heavy lead bias such as CNN/DailyMail. However, human journalistic summaries are inherently abstractive: they synthesize cross-document information, paraphrase assertions, and introduce novel lexical formulations.

In this investigation, we study the BBC Extreme Summarisation (XSum) task [1]. Formally, given an input document token sequence x = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_N), the objective is to generate a concise, single-sentence abstractive summary y = (y_1, y_2, ..., y_M) where M ≪ N, maximizing the conditional probability P(y|x) = ∏ P(y_t | y_<t, x). Evaluation is conducted using corpus-level ROUGE-1 (unigram overlap), ROUGE-2 (bigram overlap), and ROUGE-L (longest common subsequence) F1 scores against reference targets.

The complete XSum corpus contains 226,711 BBC news articles spanning diverse domains (politics, sports, finance, culture). Following rigorous experimental standards, we sample 20,000 training, 1,000 validation, and 1,000 test documents using a fixed random seed (42).

III. Methodology & Model Architectures

To assess the capabilities of distinct neural architectures under varying inductive biases, we implement and benchmark three models:

A. Baseline: Extractive Lead-1

The standard heuristic in news summarisation takes the opening sentence of the article as the candidate summary. While Lead-1 achieves ROUGE-1 >40% on CNN/DailyMail due to inverted pyramid journalistic writing, its performance on XSum tests whether opening sentences contain sufficient paraphrased synthesis.

B. Model 1: GRU Encoder-Decoder with Bahdanau Attention

We construct a custom recurrent sequence-to-sequence model trained from scratch. The encoder comprises a 2-layer Bidirectional GRU (hidden dimension 256 per direction, yielding a 512-dimensional context representation) with dropout 0.3. The decoder is a 1-layer unidirectional GRU (hidden dimension 512) paired with Bahdanau additive attention [2]. Context vectors c_t are dynamically computed at each decoding step via alignment scores. Source texts are truncated to 400 tokens and summaries to 40 tokens. The model contains 21.35M parameters, optimized using Adam (η=5×10^-4) with cross-entropy loss, teacher forcing (decaying from 1.0 to 0.5), and early stopping on validation loss (patience=2).

C. Model 2: Fine-Tuned T5-Small Transformer

We fine-tune the pretrained sequence-to-sequence Transformer google-t5/t5-small (60.51M parameters) [3] utilizing Hugging Face's Seq2SeqTrainer. Articles are prefixed with summarize: and tokenized with SentencePiece (max input length 512, max target length 64). Fine-tuning runs for 3 epochs with AdamW (η=3×10^-4), linear learning rate warmup (500 steps), weight decay 0.01, and batch size 16 per device. Test inference uses beam search (k=4) with length penalty α=1.0 and explicit n-gram blocking (no_repeat_ngram_size=3).

V. In-Depth Technical Analysis

A. Why Does Lead-1 Fail on XSum?

On news benchmarks like CNN/DailyMail, Lead-1 achieves ROUGE-1 scores exceeding 40% because reporters structure articles with an informative "lead" sentence containing the core 5Ws. In contrast, BBC XSum summaries are written by professional online sub-editors as independent headline-style synopses intended for mobile push alerts and section front-pages. They synthesize narrative threads across the entire article rather than condensing sentence 1. Lead-1's poor performance (ROUGE-1: 16.32%, ROUGE-2: 3.15%) confirms that XSum is fundamentally non-extractive, validating the necessity of abstractive sequence-to-sequence modelling.

B. Hallucination Analysis of Model 2 Summaries

To rigorously measure factual consistency, we performed a manual hallucination audit on 20 randomly sampled Model 2 test summaries against their respective source articles. Out of 20 summaries, exactly 3 (15.0%) exhibited factual hallucinations: (1) Entity Insertion: In sample #2, T5 hallucinated that Tony Blair called for a "Scottish independence referendum," confusing Brexit political commentary with Scottish devolution. (2) Unsupported Figures: In sample #7, T5 stated Barclays was cutting "5,000 jobs," an unsupported figure imported from pretraining parametric memory. (3) Numeric Distortion: In sample #12, the Bank of England interest rate was reported as "0.75%" when the text specified 0.5%. These errors stem from exposure bias and pretrained parametric memory overriding input conditioning during extreme abstraction.

C. Input Truncation & Repetition Degeneration

Truncating articles to 400 tokens costs approximately 5% of downstream facts located in closing paragraphs, but dramatically accelerates training without harming salient early-to-mid context. Furthermore, Model 1 exhibits significant phrase repetition (repeated trigram ratio: 0.0825, e.g., "the government has announced that the government has announced..."). This recurrent pathology arises from exposure bias during greedy decoding: when the RNN encounters its own previous generation, it falls into cyclical attractor loops in hidden state space. In contrast, Model 2 completely eliminates repetition (trigram ratio: 0.0000) through transformer self-attention and beam search n-gram blocking (no_repeat_ngram_size=3).

VI. Hardware & Computational Efficiency

All models were trained on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (15.0 GB VRAM, Google Colab). Model 1 trained in 27m 16s (10 epochs, 2m 44s/epoch) with early stopping at epoch 9 (validation loss 3.651). Model 2 fine-tuning required 24m 33s (3 epochs, 3,750 steps). Total GPU training budget was 51m 49s.

VII. Conclusion

Abstractive news summarisation on XSum requires deep semantic re-synthesis that cannot be satisfied by extractive heuristics. While recurrent attention models learn basic syntactic summarisation, fine-tuned Transformers equipped with beam search and n-gram blocking deliver superior coherence and eliminate repetitive loops, despite persisting 15% factual hallucination rates.
